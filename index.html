        How value is worked out

        Value is how much food each dollar buys: the item's energy (kJ or Calories), protein or serve weight divided by its price. It is a cost-of-food lens, not a health rating. For scale, an average adult needs about 8,700 kJ (2,080 Cal) a day — the reference Australian nutrition panels use.

        Only published figures are ranked. Many items have no published energy: every Subway and Domino's item, Pizza Hut's deals and most of GYG's menu. Those items are counted and listed under the ranking rather than dropped, and they are never given an estimated figure — a guess divided by a low price would make cheap items look like bargains they may not be. Two gaps are filled because the answer is exact: Calories and kilojoules are converted into each other (1 Cal = 4.184 kJ), and an item with no figure borrows it from the same item's listing under the other order type. Those figures are marked.

        Protein and weight have narrow coverage. Only the chains listed under each option publish them, so those rankings compare those chains only.

        Food only hides drinks (including coffee and shakes), sauces and dips, and add-ons that are only sold with another item. Charity donations and toys are never ranked. Identical listings are shown once.
